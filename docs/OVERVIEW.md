# What this research is trying to learn

A theory of everything would need to connect the universe's fundamental interactions in one consistent physical account. Naming that goal does not complete it. The work here takes smaller questions seriously because they can be calculated and checked.

## How hidden heavy particles enter a low-energy calculation

The neutrino branch assumes heavy states with stated masses and couplings. Its selected tree kernel keeps their full momentum dependence. A shorter low-energy expansion is easier to use, but it drops terms. The exact expression lets us calculate what was lost.

For one nonzero pole and a probe-to-heavy-mass ratio r below one, keeping the leading term gives relative kernel error r squared. Including the next term gives r to the fourth power. At r = one-half, these are 25 percent and 6.25 percent. These percentages belong to the kernel approximation; they are not uncertainties on measured neutrino masses.

## Why the loop claim was corrected

The old shortcut treated distinct field pairings as though one external momentum factor described both. One pairing actually carries momentum circulating through the loop. The published correction withdrew the stronger physical coefficient claim while preserving the independent tree results.

The later checkpoint evaluated the stated seed with both pairings retained. Its additional momentum and Higgs-mass structures cannot be recovered by changing one multiplier. Completing their physical interpretation still requires the full operator and sourced-equation reduction.

## Why two scalar numbers cannot reveal a whole spectrum

The separate scalar branch has a continuous spectrum reaching zero under inherited assumptions. Its low-energy coefficients exist only up to the threshold permitted by its spectral density. Fractional powers or logarithms can follow.

A positive single-pole model can match the static value and first slope and provide a lower bound in the Euclidean setting. It does not reconstruct the continuum. The scalar positivity argument also cannot automatically be applied to complex Majorana residues.

## Why cancellation does not mean the absence of all dynamics

Two equal-mass neutrino contributions can cancel in the selected lepton-number-violating kernel. In a prescribed time-dependent mass example, balanced heavy particle–antiparticle pairs can still be produced. A one-percent mass dip gives a rigorously positive probability for one specified mode even though the mass stays positive.

That example conserves lepton number and creates no net asymmetry. Its external driver is an input, not a derived cosmic energy source. It answers a limited question about which conclusions a stationary coefficient can support.

## What would count as stronger progress?

Complete the remaining physical calculation, fix a proposed model's inputs before testing unseen data and translate the result into a measurable quantity with uncertainties. A successful replay is a necessary check on the implementation. It does not replace those physical steps.
