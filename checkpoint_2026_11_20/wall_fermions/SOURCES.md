# Published comparisons for fermions on an HDblast wall

Two ingredients of the calculation have direct external checks. The spinor harmonics fix the number of states to sum on a round three-sphere. An exact solution on a prescribed exponentially expanding background tests the evolution and finite-time particle projection at each end of an interval. Neither input requires identifying the charged gas with a sterile neutrino. [Source and search ledger](SOURCES.json)

## Spectrum, evolution and particle definition

Cortez and colleagues derive the spinor-mode expansion on a closed Friedmann–Lemaître–Robertson–Walker spacetime. For a unit round three-sphere the intrinsic operator has opposite-sign eigenvalues of magnitude $k_n=n+3/2$, $n=0,1,\ldots$, each with degeneracy $d_n=(n+1)(n+2)$. Their two-component mode labels run from $1$ through $d_n$. For the present one-species Majorana count, the two spin sectors give $2d_n$ possible individual excitations at each energy. A separate antiparticle copy would double count a self-conjugate field. In the large-radius limit, the count approaches the two-spin flat density $2\int d^3p/(2\pi)^3$. [Cortez et al., Sec. II, Eqs. (2.3)–(2.6)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1603.00037v2)

Chung and colleagues derive the rescaled mode Hamiltonian $H_k^{(\eta)}=k\sigma_x+ma\sigma_z$ in conformal time. Its eigenvectors rotate when $ma$ varies. In flat space the comoving momentum $k$ is continuous. The closed-wall calculation inserts $k_n$ and changes to wall proper time using $dt=a\,d\eta$. A finite-time projection specifies both the initial state and the readout basis. Computing stress-energy involves separate ultraviolet subtractions. Landete, Navarro-Salas and Torrentí derive the fermionic adiabatic expansion and identify the orders through four required for stress-energy. [Chung et al., Eqs. (2)–(6)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1109.2524v1) · [Landete et al., Secs. II–III](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1311.4958v2)

## An exact finite-interval control

Boonpan gives a two-component de Sitter solution in terms of Whittaker functions and expresses occupation as a projector onto the instantaneous energy eigenspace. Set $x=-k\eta=k/(aH)>0$ and $\nu=m/H$. For $H$ constant the normalized positive-frequency solution in the $k\sigma_x+ma\sigma_z$ convention is
$$
u(x)=\frac1{\sqrt2}\begin{pmatrix}W_{0,1/2-i\nu}(-2ix)\\W_{0,1/2+i\nu}(-2ix)\end{pmatrix}.
$$
With $u=(u_1,u_2)^T$, the column $(-u_2^*,u_1^*)^T$ is its orthogonal solution, since the Hermitian two-level Hamiltonian is traceless. Use these two columns as a matrix $F(x)$. In this form the exact propagator $F(x_f)F(x_i)^\dagger$ also evolves a state **prepared at finite time**. Prepare the normalized negative eigenvector of $\sigma_x+(\nu/x_i)\sigma_z$ at $x_i=8$, and take the squared overlap with the positive eigenvector at $x_f=0.25$. [Boonpan, Eqs. (2.11), (4.1)–(4.4), (A.2) and (A.16)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.00410v1) · [Journal version](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.aop.2026.170733)

The Whittaker evaluation used 50-digit working precision for the two special functions. Direct integration of $d\psi/dx=i[\sigma_x+(\nu/x)\sigma_z]\psi$ with DOP853 agreed within $8\times10^{-14}$ in absolute probability. The wall mode program also tests an exponential scale factor in proper time against these values. This control exercises a known background and the finite-time occupation convention. ## Which inputs can be transferred to a carrier calculation?

Hashiba and Yokoyama explicitly study the gravitational production of hierarchical Majorana neutrinos and their subsequent Yukawa decays. Their Eqs. (7)–(11) use an inflation-to-kination transition and its computed production spectrum. This establishes a direct antecedent for the proposed mechanism. Its quantitative dependence on that expansion history precludes transferring the fitted abundance to a wall. Pilaftsis gives the open lepton–scalar phase-space dependence and the massless-doublet total-width normalization in a specified Yukawa convention. The TOE benchmark has its own masses, charges, flavor assignments and accessible decay channels. A further route explored in 2026 uses coherent scalar oscillations to drive fermion production; it requires an explicit scalar–fermion coupling and background. [Hashiba and Yokoyama, Eqs. (7)–(11)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1905.12423v3) · [Pilaftsis, Eqs. (4.2)–(4.3) and text following (4.21)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/hep-ph/9707235v1) · [Scalar-driven fermionic preheating, Sec. II](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.07326v3)

For the present calculation the archived matter-free trace supplies the radius, its expansion rate and the wall clock. The separate charged-matter simulation supplies the numerical target, its own time and radius endpoints, the force convention $Q=|q|/(\sqrt3\,mL)$ in natural units, and the specified initial rest density in wall-tension units. In particular, the HDblast paper distinguishes the externally supplied cold precursors, the distribution of quantum scalar excitations and the assumed conversion into mobile charges. A dense neutral-background mode calculation does not evolve the current, drag, scattering, electric field or charged-particle distribution of the coupled run. The published four-singlet TOE model specifies the Yukawa and the one-loop pole splitting. The two calculations still need a common charged-matter theory to relate those inputs to mobility and internal energy.
