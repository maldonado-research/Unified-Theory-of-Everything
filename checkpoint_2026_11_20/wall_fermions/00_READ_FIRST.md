# Can particles produced on the HDblast wall supply its charged gas?

HDblast supplies two concrete inputs for a test of the TOE matter model. One is a simulated closed wall that contracts, bounces and expands. The other is a gas-loaded run that specifies a mobile charge population. We can evolve the proposed neutral particles on the first geometry and independently ask how many charged states could realize the gas on the second. Throughout, counts refer to individual particles on the entire spatial three-sphere. [HDblast publication](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.24405438) · [TOE singlet model](../../checkpoint_2026_11_19/uv_completion/MATCHING.md)

## What the comparison establishes

1. **The cold charged gas rules out a class of microscopic models.** At the mobility switch, three free, equal-mass charged Dirac species obeying the prescribed charge response can supply less than one ten-millionth of the required carriers at exactly zero kinetic energy, for any mass and a local coupling $\alpha<1$. Even after allowing mean kinetic energy up to 10% of rest energy, the mass $mL=1$ allows about 4,014 carriers, while the fluid requires more than 2.85 billion. For $mL=4$ the corresponding figures are 68,008 and over 11.42 billion. These are Pauli state-count limits, independent of production history. [Derivation, assumptions and numerical tests](BRIDGE.md)
2. **The 10% test admits an explicit heavier population.** Every $0<mL\le1970$ is excluded under $\alpha<1$. An explicit distribution at $mL=2000$ fits both the number and the mean energy at $\alpha=100/101$. If $\alpha\le0.1$, every $mL\le6240$ is excluded and a distribution at 6500 works at $\alpha=10/101$. These masses are in inverse curvature-length units. Passing the test establishes that the states exist. The model would still need to populate them at the required time. [Bounds and constructed distributions](BRIDGE.json)
3. **The tested neutral-singlet decay source falls short.** With the benchmark mass choice $mL=1$ and Yukawa strength $|g|=1$, a cohort prepared from the heavy singlet occupation at the bounce yields an estimated 0.00371–0.00442 prompt massive charged scalars and the same number of massless charged leptons by the reference mobility time. Even its upper yield is over $6.46\times10^{11}$ times too small to supply the trial gas's required massive-carrier count at the late perturbative threshold. The decay estimate uses the neutral geometry, local vacuum decay, time dilation and a specified one-time preparation. It does not evolve a coupled reheating history. [Widths, shell estimates and charge accounting](CARRIERS.md)
4. **The wall evolution makes a mass comparison possible.** In the instantaneous particle basis at $t=11.03911$ the neutral $ML=4$ species outnumbers the $ML=1$ species. At turnaround the lighter species outnumbers the heavier, which still carries more excitation energy. Shells 0 through 2048 were evolved numerically. Bounds on every higher shell preserve both number orderings on the specified interpolated wall. On the expanding slice the total number converges but the unrenormalized instantaneous excitation energy diverges logarithmically. A prediction for the gravitational stress would require a renormalized stress tensor in a physically specified state. [Resolved spectra](BAND.md) · [High-frequency bounds](TAIL.md) · [External and analytic controls](CALIBRATION.md)

## Interpreting the two histories

The neutral run uses a free minimally coupled Majorana spectator, initially in the negative instantaneous eigenspace at $t=0$, on the archived wall trajectory. Its bounce is at $t=0.867705$ and turnaround at $12.421633$. The two mass assignments $ML=1,4$ are choices motivated by the pole ordering of the TOE benchmark. Its lightest odd state is stable against individual decay. Its heavier state can decay into charged and neutral channels. Occupations on different slices are separate readouts of a coherent state. Turning a readout into an incoherent cohort is an added preparation in the decay model. [Mode calculation](MODES.md) · [TOE pole ordering](../two_particle_cut/POLE_THRESHOLD.md)

The charged run already includes a cold precursor and switches on its mobility at $t=10.5017$. Only this run sets the number target and the instantaneous magnetic field for the charged Dirac calculation. The charged scalar from the singlet decay and the charged Dirac candidates have different statistics. The scalar yield has its own number shortfall. The Dirac capacity calculation tests an alternative microscopic assignment. Neither calculation supplies a collision rate for the coupled gas. A direct test of another assignment would need its charge, mass, initial distribution, and a mechanism that produces and cools the required population by the switch. [HDblast gas dynamics](https://github.com/maldonado-research/HDblast/blob/030a4a0b4c2e65fdbce15fbe76b458a7addffdc9/research/HDBLAST_CHARGED_MATTER_HEATING_20261120/coupled/COUPLED.md) · [Literature and archive review](SOURCES.md)

A short analytical check also applies to a **newly prepared neutral mode** on the late monotone charged-run geometry, neglecting any other force. For $k=n+3/2$, its Hamiltonian direction has angle $\theta=\arctan[k/(aML)]$. In the instantaneous basis the transition amplitude obeys $|d(\arcsin\sqrt P)/d\eta|\le |\theta'|/2$. If $P$ starts at zero, the total angle change bounds $P$. Maximizing over $k/(ML)$ at $\sqrt{a_i a_f}$ gives $P\le(\sqrt r-1)^2/[2(1+r)]$, $r=a_f/a_i$. The interval from the first 90% intrinsic-energy readout to the maximum radius gives $7.661\times10^{-6}$ per mode. Summing this uniform per-mode bound over an infinite spectrum would give no population estimate. [Endpoint inputs](HD_INPUTS.json) · [Check](BRIDGE.json)

## Reproduction and data credit

The repository's full replay uses Python 3.12, its pinned numerical packages, and a C++17 compiler. From the repository root run the following command. It includes the six wall checks, recomputes their outputs, and compares them with the stored results. [Verification instructions](../../docs/VERIFICATION.md)

```sh
python verify.py --full
```

For a quick independent check of the charged-state calculation, change to this directory and run `python -I -B -O check_bridge.py`. Optional arguments `--source-trace /path/to/trace.csv.gz --source-gas /path/to/CHARGED.json` compare the extracts with the original archives. The geometry file retains all 29,558 rows and the original decimal tokens of the first three of 17 columns, with LF line endings and a new gzip container. The JSON input records selected parameters and rows from the gas simulation's primary run. The source digests and the uncompressed geometry digest are recorded alongside the extracts.
