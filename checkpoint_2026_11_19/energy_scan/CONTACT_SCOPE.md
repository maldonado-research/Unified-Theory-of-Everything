# Which contact coefficient the energy scan measures

In the unbroken gaugeless theory, fix the lepton flavor, take both external left-handed leptons massless and in the same weak component, and give the two identical spin-zero particles squared mass $a>0$. Treat the scalars as stable external states of the cut. A local four-point tree with at most two derivatives then gives two distinguishable energy shapes at fixed mass. With the spin determinant removed they are $1$ and $s$. The derivative seed in the action contributes to the slope. Other local operators with the same field content can contribute to that slope as well. A scan that profiles an arbitrary constant can constrain a specified nonzero **total slope** only after the other side of the cut and the other absorptive contributions have been fixed. ## Reduction of four-dimensional local polynomials

Use incoming lepton momenta $p_1,p_2$, outgoing scalar momenta $k_1,k_2$, $P=p_1+p_2=k_1+k_2$, $s=P^2$, $t=(p_1-k_1)^2$, $u=(p_1-k_2)^2$. Then $s+t+u=2a$. The factor for the two incoming spinors is $B=u_1^T\epsilon u_2$, with commuting external wavefunctions and $\epsilon_{12}=1$. The reversed subamplitude uses the conjugate determinant and the external-state sign fixed in the tree convention above.

The product of two undotted Weyl spinors has a scalar and a symmetric pair of undotted indices. The latter is the self-dual Lorentz two-form, up to the convention for duality. At zero derivatives only the scalar can enter. A single vector carries an unpaired dotted index, so a local scalar bilinear with one derivative is absent. At two derivatives one can multiply $B$ by a metric contraction $q\cdot r$, or contract the two-form with $q_{[\mu}r_{\nu]}$. Its dual is proportional to the same chiral tensor. These choices exhaust the Lorentz contractions with two momentum insertions. Integration by parts and momentum conservation allow $q,r$ to be drawn from the four external momenta. The conclusion assumes Lorentz-invariant vacuum coefficients and no external fields or polarization vectors beyond the four specified here.

Define the ordered spin chain $Q(q,r)=u_1^T\epsilon\,\sigma(q)\bar\sigma(r)u_2$, with $\sigma(q)=q^0-\boldsymbol q\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma$, $\bar\sigma(q)=q^0+\boldsymbol q\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma$. Its symmetric part is $(q\cdot r)B$, and its antisymmetric part represents the chiral tensor. Since $\sigma(p_i)=u_i u_i^\dagger$ at the usual wavefunction normalization, $Q(p_1,r)=Q(q,p_2)=0$ on shell. Momentum conservation and the Clifford identity give the following ordered values of $Q(q,r)/B$.

| $q\backslash r$ | $p_1$ | $p_2$ | $k_1$ | $k_2$ |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| $p_1$ | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| $p_2$ | $s$ | 0 | $a-u$ | $a-t$ |
| $k_1$ | $a-t$ | 0 | $a$ | $-t$ |
| $k_2$ | $a-u$ | 0 | $-u$ | $a$ |

For example, $Q(k_1,k_2)=Q(k_1,p_1)-aB=(2k_1\cdot p_1-a)B=-tB$. The metric contractions are already included as half sums of ordered chains. Hence before projecting onto identical states the reduced polynomial has the form $c_0+c_s s+c_t t+c_u u+c_a a$, allowing constant mass spurions in its coefficients. Under either $k_1\leftrightarrow k_2$ or $p_1\leftrightarrow p_2$, $t\leftrightarrow u$. Bose symmetry requires the symmetric part. Fermi antisymmetry gives the same condition because $B$ itself changes sign. The only surviving combination of $t,u$ is $t+u=2a-s$. The fixed-mass contact amplitude is therefore $B[P_0+P_1s]$, with complex $P_0,P_1$. Both shapes occur. For example $B$ and $(k_1\cdot k_2)B=(s/2-a)B$ are linearly independent across two energies.

A tensor can feed this slope. For $T(q,r)=[Q(q,r)-Q(r,q)]/2$, $T(k_1,k_2)=(u-t)B/2$ drops out of the Bose projection, while $T(p_1-p_2,k_1+k_2)=-sB$ survives both exchanges. The explicit reduction covers operators with derivatives on the leptons, on the scalars, on mixed pairs, and on a single leg. Taking an external $-\Box\phi$ to $a\phi$ or using the free lepton wave equation here computes this on-shell contact matrix element. Acting on the whole lepton-scalar composite gives $t$ or $u$. An off-shell operator reduction, or a field redefinition with interactions present, requires the corresponding source, exchange, and induced vertices. ## Normalization and degeneracy

In the convention with $-B_{34}^*$ removed from the electron-side tree, the literal positive-action coefficients give $2C_5+C_7(t+u)$. Dividing by the two scalar assignments gives

$$
F(s)=C_5+C_7(a-s/2).
$$

For the more general projected contact, write the same normalized polynomial as $F(s)=c+d(a-s/2)$. The fitted constant $c$ absorbs every energy-independent contribution at fixed $a$. The dimension-five coefficient has mass dimension $-1$, and $d$ has dimension $-3$. The scan conditional on $d\ne0$ can use the seed formula with $C_7\to d$. In the restricted two-operator action $d=C_7$. For illustration, a four-point structure with normalized coefficient $D\, k_1\cdot k_2$ gives $d=C_7-D$. It can be realized with the weak contractions of the Weinberg operator and one covariant derivative on each Higgs, with $D$ defined at the matrix-element level to absorb the vertex signs and factors. Likewise $p_1\cdot p_2=s/2$ supplies a slope and a scalar-leg square supplies a constant. These examples refer to representatives before any basis choice. A change among representatives need not supply a new independent Wilson coefficient. If independent matching permits the compensating slope, $D=C_7$ cancels the derivative contribution at every energy even for nonzero literal $C_7$.

## Where the scan bound applies

* A change of weak components can distinguish the two scalar routes. More general flavor or helicity assignments require their own exchange projection. At dimension nine the local structure $B(t-u)^2$ is exchange-allowed and has even angular dependence. Even an angle-independent $s^2 B$ term provides a new energy shape. For two distinct scan energies $s_1,s_2>4a$, a polynomial proportional to $(s-s_1)(s-s_2)$ has nonzero linear coefficient and vanishes at both. The two-energy floor requires a controlled amplitude truncation.
* A light exchange propagator has denominators such as $1/(t-m^2)$. A loop form factor can have logarithms or thresholds. Analyticity in a neighborhood of a scan point alone places no finite degree bound on either. The contact projection uses a local polynomial of degree at most two in momenta. The reciprocal-rate identity uses the full absorptive amplitude at the order retained. Other discontinuities can interfere destructively with the two-scalar contribution. A Hermitian *four-lepton contact tree* shifts only the dispersive part at that order.
* The rate bound squares the specified retained amplitude. In a strict inverse-heavy-scale expansion with unsuppressed dimension-five amplitude, the square of a dimension-seven amplitude is of order $\Lambda^{-6}$. Interference of dimension five with dimension nine has the same order. The positive squared-amplitude floor needs the stated amplitude model or a hierarchy that controls all omitted terms at the required accuracy. Profiling minimizes over the unknown constant and supplies no estimate for the omitted amplitudes.
* Two stable scalars give the two-particle phase space used in the cut. The benchmark $m_N=(\sqrt a,4\sqrt a)$ closes scalar decays to these sterile states and a massless lepton in the specified sector, including zero phase space at equality. With open decay channels the fixed-order cut still characterizes the selected free-propagator calculation. An observable with appreciable scalar widths generally requires the spectral distribution and the stable decay-product channels, with a new threshold and normalization analysis.
