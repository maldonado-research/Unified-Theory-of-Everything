## The two-lepton, two-Higgs amplitude

Lehman's dimension-seven SMEFT construction, §5, Eqs. (5.11) and (5.12), displays two distinct weak-index contractions with two lepton doublets, two Higgs fields and two covariant derivatives. Zhang's §2 and Table 1 display the dimension-five Weinberg interaction, derivative operators and additional representatives in redundant coordinates. The papers classify operators using Standard Model field content. The present projection selects one external flavor, chirality and weak component, and its loop also contains retained sterile fields. Several operator coefficients can therefore contribute to the same measured contact slope. The sign and normalization of the specific project seed follow from its literal action. [Lehman, arXiv:1410.4193v2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1410.4193v2) · [Zhang, arXiv:2306.03008v2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2306.03008v2)

Durieux and Machado, §§II–IV, organize local contact amplitudes by their minimal helicity spinor structures and dress them with momentum invariants. Momentum conservation, Schouten identities and identical-particle statistics reduce the resulting list. Their enumeration uses massless external particles. For the two massive equal scalars here the mass shell gives the additional fixed invariant a, and the reduction must impose the scalar interchange on the actual massive kinematics. [Durieux & Machado, arXiv:1912.08827v3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1912.08827v3)

## Squared amplitudes and profiling an unknown constant

Boughezal, Mereghetti and Petriello compare squared dimension-six amplitudes with dimension-eight interference in a Drell–Yan cross section computed through inverse fourth power of the cutoff. Section IV shows how both enter constraints on the differential rate. The same order-counting issue arises here with different powers. At fixed masses, if an amplitude contains terms of orders Λ⁻¹, Λ⁻³ and Λ⁻⁵, the square of the Λ⁻³ term and interference between the Λ⁻¹ and Λ⁻⁵ terms both scale as Λ⁻⁶. Squaring the two specified interaction amplitudes is meaningful within the declared model. A general EFT prediction to that inverse-scale order requires the other contributing amplitudes. The Drell–Yan fits provide no numerical input to the present bound. [Boughezal, Mereghetti & Petriello, arXiv:2106.05337v3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2106.05337v3)

Dette and Holland-Letz, §2, formulate approximate experimental designs as distributions of positive exposure weights and use an information matrix for a linear parameter contrast. The two columns consisting of a constant and an energy-dependent term give a useful mathematical comparison here. Minimizing a weighted quadratic form over the constant leaves its weighted slope variance. The resulting lower bound is deterministic. Translating it into an experimental sensitivity requires a likelihood, efficiencies, backgrounds and energy response. [Dette & Holland-Letz, arXiv:0911.3801v1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0911.3801v1)
