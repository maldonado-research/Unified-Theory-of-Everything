# Dispersive insertions, three-particle cuts and charged-scalar decays

19 November 2026. The published comparisons below test the normalization of a charged-scalar self-energy, its insertion inside a box and the threshold of the resulting three-particle cut. The numerical application uses the masses and couplings of the [four-singlet benchmark](../uv_completion/MATCHING.md).

## Width and subtraction at the scalar pole

Liu, Han, Jin and Li give the width for a charged component of an inert scalar doublet decaying to a charged lepton and one singlet Majorana fermion. Their Eq. (5.6), with the charged-lepton mass neglected, reads $\Gamma=|y|^2 m_\eta(1-M_i^2/m_\eta^2)^2/(16\pi)$. For virtual scalar mass $\sqrt U$ and squared fermion mass $B$, $\sqrt U\,\Gamma$ gives the positive absorptive weight $|y|^2(U-B)^2/(16\pi U)$ above threshold. Each specified flavor and charge contributes once. The benchmark uses this kinematic identity for its own interaction and masses. [Liu et al., Eq. (5.6)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2207.07382v2)

Aleksejevs shows how to replace a self-energy subloop by a dispersion integral inside a remaining one-loop function. The massive-vector example in Eq. (15) subtracts both the value and first derivative at the pole. Two powers of the external inverse propagator then cancel the two adjacent propagators. Figure 1 includes box insertions, and Eqs. (12)–(13) express the remaining loop in terms of one-loop functions. Aleksejevs and Barkanova give the general multiply-subtracted spectral kernel in Eqs. (5)–(6). A stable charged scalar of pole mass squared $a$ has the same subtraction algebra. In the pole-residue convention this leaves a continuum proportional to $\sqrt U\,\Gamma/[\pi(U-a)^2]$. When $B=a$ the two powers of $U-B$ in the width cancel the two powers in this denominator. The coincident endpoint requires treating that cancellation before numerical evaluation. Combining these published relations fixes the normalization and endpoint behavior independently of the four-lepton calculation. [Aleksejevs, Eqs. (12)–(16)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1804.08914v6) · [Aleksejevs and Barkanova, Eqs. (5)–(6)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1905.07936v1)

## Three-particle phase space and the massive boxes

Bauberger, Berends, Böhm and Buza evaluate the three-particle discontinuity of the massive sunset in Eq. (4) as an integral of two two-particle discontinuities. The pair invariant ranges from $(m_2+m_3)^2$ to $(\sqrt s-m_1)^2$. Kersevan and Richter-Wąs derive the recursive Lorentz-invariant phase-space measure with angular dependence retained in Eq. (19). In this application the particles carry masses $m,0,\sqrt B$. The resulting invariant-mass window is $B\le U\le(\sqrt s-m)^2$, and it opens at $s=(m+\sqrt B)^2$. For $B=m^2,16m^2,100m^2,400m^2$, only the first channel contributes to this cut at the three benchmark energies. The dispersive real part can still sample all four continua. [Bauberger et al., Eqs. (4)–(5)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/hep-ph/9409388v2) · [Kersevan and Richter-Wąs, Eq. (19)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/hep-ph/0405248v2)

Song and Freitas use one-loop functions under a dispersion integral to evaluate two-loop electroweak boxes. In Eqs. (2)–(3) their scalar bubble discontinuity in four dimensions is $\mathrm{Im}\,B_0/\pi=\sqrt{\lambda(U,m_1^2,m_2^2)}/U$, with $\lambda$ the Källén function. Taking $m_1=0$ and $m_2^2=B$ gives the phase-space factor $(U-B)/U$. The chiral Yukawa spin sum supplies the second $U-B$ in the width above. For the reciprocal flavor process, Cannoni, Kolb and Panella give explicit one-loop Majorana charged-Goldstone boxes with the mass product and $D_0(s,t)+D_0(s,u)$ in Eq. (4). Their Goldstone contribution forms part of a complete electroweak amplitude. Here the internal scalar is a physical field of an unbroken gaugeless model. The continuum insertion carries the same external spinor and flavor contractions as its parent box. [Song and Freitas, Eqs. (2)–(3)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2101.00308v2) · [Cannoni, Kolb and Panella, Eq. (4)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/hep-ph/0209120v2)

## Recent numerical work and the author's public projects

Two October 2026 preprints give related numerical comparisons. Kermanschah evaluates a two-loop fermion-bubble contribution to quark-antiquark annihilation into several photons using local ultraviolet and threshold subtractions. Dölz and colleagues study numerical quadrature for one-loop integrals and give a physical-region triangle example after treating its threshold singularity. These illustrate independent integration strategies. Their external states, mass assignments and coupling normalizations differ from those in the present benchmark. The benchmark calculation tests a specific renormalized insertion and its physical cut. The general dispersion construction and recursive phase space have published precedents. [Kermanschah](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.35655v1) · [Dölz et al., §§4.2 and 5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.41872v1)
