# One-loop stability of the electron-flavor cancellation in the literature

19 November 2026. The closest precedents give both the finite threshold diagram and its leading result in the heavy-mass expansion. The four-singlet benchmark also keeps the scalar mass finite, so its zero-momentum Green function and the leading dimension-five Wilson coefficient have different domains of application.

## Matching at a common scale

Zhang and Zhou integrate out nondegenerate Majorana singlets in the unbroken Standard Model. Let $W_i=Y_{ei}^2/M_i$ and use their $V=\lambda(H^\dagger H)^2$ quartic normalization. Equations (47)–(48) give the one-loop dimension-five vertex coefficient, after modified minimal subtraction, as
$$
 16\pi^2\,\delta C^{(5)}_{ee}\big|_{\mathrm{vertex}}
 =\sum_i W_i\left[2\lambda(1+L_i)+\frac{g_1^2+g_2^2}{4}(1+3L_i)\right],
 \qquad L_i=\ln(\mu^2/M_i^2).
$$
Equation (88) adds the canonical field corrections acting on the **sum** of the tree-level coefficients. In the present flavor blocks the electron row and column of that sum vanish, so each of those terms vanishes at its $ee$ entry. For $W_1=-W_2=y_e^2/M$ and $M_2=2M_1=2M$, the vertex expression reduces to $\big[2\lambda+3(g_1^2+g_2^2)/4\big]\,y_e^2\ln4/M$. This gives a positive quartic correction for real positive $W_1$ and $\lambda$. The project sets $g_1=g_2=0$. Its calculation with a finite scalar mass retains additional powers of $m^2/M_i^2$. The published Wilson coefficient tests the leading term in that expansion. The diagrammatic matching equation of Ohlsson and Pernow separately displays external-field and vertex contributions in a calculation assuming degenerate heavy singlets. [Zhang & Zhou, Eqs. (47), (48), (88)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2107.12133v3) · [Ohlsson & Pernow, §5.9 and Eq. (85)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2201.00840v2)

## Running between unequal thresholds

Antusch and collaborators draw the scalar-quartic graph with the full heavy propagator next to its effective-theory graph in Fig. 3. Their Table 1 distinguishes the running of an already integrated-out contribution from that of the remaining singlet exchange. Their Appendix D writes the quartic potential with $\lambda_{\rm A}/4$ multiplying $(H^\dagger H)^2$. Kersten and Smirnov specialize this difference to a cancellation at the larger of two unequal singlet masses. Their Eq. (34) exhibits the Higgs and electroweak-gauge terms and Eq. (35) the intervening mass logarithm. The mechanism by which such a cancellation can fail across thresholds has therefore been known since at least these analyses. [Antusch et al., Table 1, Fig. 3 and Appendix D](https://arxiv.org/pdf/hep-ph/0501272v3) · [Kersten & Smirnov, §2.5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0705.3221v2)

Di Zhang includes Weinberg-operator feedback into the Yukawa equations while some singlets are still dynamical. For the sum $K=C_5+Y M_N^{-1}Y^T$, Eq. (11) contains the source $(\alpha_2-\alpha_1)C_5$ in $16\pi^2\,dK/d\ln\mu$. Here $\alpha_2-\alpha_1=4\lambda+3(g_1^2+g_2^2)/2$. Between $2M$ and $M$, the heavier electron singlet has supplied $C_{5,ee}=-y_e^2/M$ at tree level. Integrating the source downwards gives the same one-loop logarithm as common-scale matching. Running above both thresholds follows a different equation. The massless-neutrino result in that article concerns the rank of a specified seesaw mass matrix. Here the electron couples to **both** poles and their signed inverse moments cancel. Below both thresholds the pure Standard Model Weinberg equation contains $4\lambda C_5$ at one loop. These statements concern running and leading heavy-mass matching. Physical neutrino masses after electroweak symmetry breaking also receive the neutral-scalar and Z-boson contributions analyzed by Grimus and Lavoura. Their Eq. (53) and the following paragraph discuss accidental vanishing of the tree mass. [Di Zhang, Eqs. (7)–(11) and §V](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2405.18017v3) · [Ibarra, Leister & Zhang, Eq. (20)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2411.08011v1) · [Grimus & Lavoura, Eq. (53)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/hep-ph/0207229v2)
