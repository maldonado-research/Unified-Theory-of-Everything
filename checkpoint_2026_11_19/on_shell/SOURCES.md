# Literature and archive checks for the on-shell cut

19 November 2026. The comparison concerns the discontinuity across the physical two-scalar threshold. Let the equal scalar squared mass be `a>0`, the exchanged sterile squared mass be `b=m²`, and the pair invariant be `s`.

## A threshold check from a 17 November preprint

Wang, Liu and Zhao distinguish the square-root singularity at an isolated two-particle threshold from the logarithm at a separate leading Landau singularity. Section III.B, Eqs. (31)–(33), provides a direct check. If the uncut propagator denominator has nonzero threshold value `d*`, the leading discontinuity of the scalar triangle is the leading bubble discontinuity divided by `d*` in their conventions. Their hadronic examples also show why vertex numerators must be evaluated at the cut kinematics. [Wang, Liu & Zhao, arXiv:2611.22424v1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.22424v1)

Here the two cut scalar lines have zero relative three-momentum at `s=4a`. A massless external lepton of energy √a then exchanges momentum with squared invariant `−a`, so `d*=−(a+b)`. The whole-composite derivative gives the same invariant `−a` in the numerator at threshold. The ratio is `a/(a+b)` before restoring the sterile mass, flavor weights, spin tensor and diagram normalization. Thus the common phase-space square root can have distinct mass-dependent coefficients. This tests the threshold limit without assigning an anomalous triangle singularity to the process. The unitarity calculation in this checkpoint fixes the absolute normalization.

## Cutting rules and coherent Majorana exchange

Abreu, Britto and Grönqvist specify the cut of an external channel in §4.1 and Appendix A, Eq. (A.5). In conventions with propagator `i/(q²−a+i0)`, the cut uses `2πδ(q²−a)` with its energy direction fixed across the cut. The two positive-energy scalar lines produce the ordinary two-body phase space in four dimensions. One must still supply the subamplitudes, including their spin, exchange and symmetry factors. [Abreu, Britto & Grönqvist, arXiv:1504.00206v1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1504.00206v1)

Abreu, Britto, Duhr and Gardi use `disc f=[f(s+i0)−f(s−i0)]/(2πi)` in §7.4, Eq. (7.20). Equations (7.25)–(7.26) and the surrounding discussion identify the physical one-loop channel cut and the threshold kinematics. A physical imaginary part can be read off from a discontinuity for a real-analytic scalar function. For complex Yukawa and seed tensors the two boundary values of the *kinematic function* can instead be subtracted at fixed couplings before multiplying the complex coefficients. [Abreu et al., arXiv:1702.03163v2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1702.03163v2)

Asaka and Tsuyuki give a useful exchange-amplitude comparison for `e⁻e⁻→W⁻W⁻`. Appendix A, Eqs. (A1)–(A5), displays the angular `t,u` transfers and the coherent Majorana sums with terms `Θ_eI² M_I/(t−M_I²)` and the crossed partner. Both the complex square and the mass-dependent denominator survive before squaring the amplitude. Here the cut contains two scalars and one subamplitude contains a derivative seed. The couplings, spin sums and rate of the charged-vector process cannot supply those ingredients. The later radiative study by Asaka, Ishida and Tanaka, §3, also illustrates the dependence of cancellations on the mass and renormalization assumptions in a seesaw model.
