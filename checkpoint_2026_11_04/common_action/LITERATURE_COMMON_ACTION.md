## A new matching comparison, with different mass assumptions

Wen-Feng Duan, Xuan-Zhen He, Xin-Qiang Li, Ya-Dong Yang and Xing-Bo Yuan, [Matching ν2HDM onto νSMEFT up to one-loop accuracy, arXiv:2611.02500v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.02500v1), 2 November 2026. The versioned primary body was retrieved; its introduction, model assumptions, Section 4.1 and the physical/Green-basis appendices were compared with the provider passages.

The work integrates out a heavy scalar doublet while retaining right-handed neutrinos. Section 4.1 first obtains a generally redundant one-loop action, then explicitly normalizes fermion and gauge kinetic terms, accompanies gauge-field rescaling with coupling rescaling, and expresses the result in an IBP-independent Green basis. Induced dimension-four scalar and Yukawa terms are retained alongside dimension-six operators. Reduction to the Warsaw-extended physical basis includes evanescent operators and a declared dimensional/subtraction convention. The appendices distinguish the redundant Green operators from the physical and evanescent sets.

**Use here:** this is a timely procedural comparison for collecting kinetic normalization, parameter changes and higher-operator terms in one declared action convention. It supports checking the assembled action, rather than treating a normalization term or a partial Green coefficient as the final physical coefficient. Its evanescent definitions also illustrate a remaining requirement beyond this repository's four-dimensional Grassmann/Fierz checks.

**Limit:** the model in Section 2.1.2 has Dirac neutrinos and a symmetry that forbids right-handed-neutrino Majorana masses. It does not calculate this project's retained-Majorana `C7 Y* M Y^dagger` packet, its mass-lowered terms or a dimension-seven derivative seed. Neither the paper's Wilson coefficients nor its basis relations are imported as this project's answer. A shared right-handed-neutrino field does not establish equivalent mass assumptions or power counting.

## Non-minimal sources and the quantum task that remains

Anders Eller Thomsen, [On the Renormalization Group in EFTs: On-Shell Bases, Ambiguities, and Divergences, arXiv:2512.14260v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.14260v1), 16 December 2025 according to the retrieved versioned body. The provider reports June 2026 created/updated fields; those index dates are recorded separately and are not used as the original posting date. The primary introduction, source-renormalization convention, Sections 4.1–4.2 and the dimension-five νSMEFT example were compared with selected passages.

Section 4 applies a field change inside the vacuum functional. A source coupled linearly to the old field then couples to the transformed composite field, producing non-minimal source terms. The resulting source sector is part of the renormalization of off-shell Green functions. The paper also analyzes flavor rotations and the ambiguity of individual coupling-coordinate beta functions. Its source-renormalization convention can trade wave-function normalization for source renormalization, which makes conventions essential when comparing tables.

**Use here:** an action-coordinate bridge must transport independent probes along with its fields. Reproducing an old and new Hessian at selected classical backgrounds is a useful check of that bridge, but is not a derivation of the fully renormalized quantum vacuum functional, composite-source counterterms or physical RG flow. Those further tasks require their own source definitions and subtraction prescription.

**Limit:** the paper explicitly distinguishes full off-shell Green-function renormalization from practical extraction of finite on-shell beta functions, for which it says the non-minimal sources can be omitted with minimal counterterm choices. Thus it does not justify a universal assertion that every finite beta calculation must retain every probe term. It provides a precise source-functional comparison, not this project's dimension-seven coefficient or evidence of quantum equivalence for its selected truncation.

## Retain redundant coordinates through differentiation

Di Zhang, [Two-loop Renormalization Group Equations in the νSMEFT, arXiv:2504.00792](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.00792), 2025. Canonical metadata and four provider-selected, unversioned passages were refreshed, particularly Section 6 and the conclusions; this paper was already assessed in earlier checkpoints.

The calculation works with retained right-handed neutrinos through dimension five. It illustrates that setting redundant Wilson coefficients to zero before differentiating renormalization constants can omit terms needed for pole consistency. It distinguishes flavor-covariant Yukawa and field-coordinate RG functions from flavor invariants and recommends explicit field changes when flavor ordering and higher-order contributions matter.

**Use here:** collect the action, counterterm signs, parameter inputs and redundant-coordinate dependence before applying the final projection. A first-order classical map fixes neither all required counterterms nor a physical beta function. The paper's lower-dimensional operator content and two-loop scope do not provide the present seed's total coefficient.

## Application to the present collection

The literature comparisons motivate four concrete bookkeeping rules for this checkpoint:

1. State whether each input is a loop pole or its cancelling action. Their signs differ before any field substitution is applied.
2. Choose one field/source coordinate map and apply its bridge to the reference interactions and probes; do not add coefficient tables obtained in different coordinates.
3. Record canonical dimension, explicit mass powers, field content and spurion/coupling order. For example, `C7` has mass dimension minus three, whereas `C7 Y* M Y^dagger` has dimension minus two. A retained mass permits lower-dimensional local operators, but does not make their coefficients interchangeable with a dimension-seven derivative coefficient. This is the project's dimensional analysis, not a coefficient borrowed from these references.
4. Keep independent lower-order insertions distinct from seed-loop outputs. Preserve the redundant-coordinate dependence needed for RG differentiation, then complete the physical/evanescent and source prescription before claiming physical running.

The remaining search results include finite-temperature matching, phenomenology and gravity field-redefinition studies. Their titles and abstracts did not supply an independently verified coefficient for this task. No new fundamental mathematics, empirical confirmation, finite observable, complete quantum source map or unification follows from this literature assessment.
