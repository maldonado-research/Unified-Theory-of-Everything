## 1. What the static coefficient actually determines

For fixed Yukawa matrix `Y`, an invertible complex symmetric Majorana mass matrix `M(φ)=M₀+G(φ−φ₀)`, and the convention `C₅=Y M⁻¹Yᵀ`, differentiation gives

\[
\frac{dC_5}{d\phi}=-Y M^{-1}G M^{-1}Y^T,\qquad
\frac{d^2C_5}{d\phi^2}=2Y M^{-1}G M^{-1}G M^{-1}Y^T.
\]

These are exact algebraic identities on every interval where M is invertible; matrix order must be retained. An overall matching-sign convention for C₅ changes both signs consistently. In spectral norm, if the smallest singular value of M is at least μ>0,

`||dC₅/dφ|| ≤ ||Y||² ||G||/μ²`.

If `|φ−φ₀| ||M₀⁻¹G||<1`, a sufficient convergent local mass expansion is

`M(φ)⁻¹ = Σₙ[−(φ−φ₀)M₀⁻¹G]ⁿ M₀⁻¹`.

This expansion concerns field displacement, not dynamical adiabaticity. A mass can remain nonzero while changing too quickly for derivative elimination. Conversely, the coefficient C₅ can cancel while heavy states remain dynamically relevant. Static seesaw matching through dimension seven is established prior work, and it does not itself supply a time-dependent particle-production calculation. [Elgaard-Clausen and Trott, 2017](https://arxiv.org/abs/1703.04415).

## 2. Why replacing a static mass by m(t) is not the full inverse

For a real mass in a first-order spinor operator, write `D=iγ^μ∂_μ` and `K=D−M(x)`. Its formal low-derivative inverse is

\[
K^{-1}=-M^{-1}-M^{-1}DM^{-1}
-M^{-1}DM^{-1}DM^{-1}-\cdots.
\]

Applied to a source J, the first derivative term includes

\[
-M^{-1}D(M^{-1}J)
=-M^{-2}DJ+i\gamma^\mu M^{-2}(\partial_\mu M)M^{-1}J,
\]

because `[D,M⁻¹]=−iγ^μ M⁻¹(∂_μM)M⁻¹`. This displayed form assumes flavor matrices commute with the gamma matrices; it preserves noncommuting flavor order. For general complex Majorana masses the complete Nambu/chiral mass operator must be used. This structural inverse is **not** a claim for a new Weinberg or dimension-seven coefficient: chiral projection, charge conjugation, operator reduction and matching remain necessary.

Ordinary static finite-pole identities describe translation-invariant kernels. A varying mass gives a two-time kernel, with causal boundary conditions and an initial state; it cannot generally be represented by the same function of one conserved frequency. Small external momenta relative to the heavy scale are necessary for a conventional local expansion, but not sufficient when the background carries rapid frequency content. A sufficient regime must also control the background derivatives, relevant spectral gaps, state preparation and accumulated errors over the interval. The operator series above is formal until a suitable restricted operator norm or remainder estimate is supplied; differential operators are unbounded on an unrestricted function space.

There is no general failure of effective-field-theory principles here. A state-dependent, nonlocal influence functional can retain heavy-sector memory; what fails is an unjustified **stationary local truncation**. Time-dependent initial-state effects and nonlocal terms have explicit precedents. [Collins, Holman and Ross](https://arxiv.org/abs/1208.3255). Heavy-field decoupling can remain valid with an adequate gap and appropriate adiabaticity, while violations have to respect energy conservation. [Avgoustidis et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1203.0016).

## 3. Exact two-level benchmark and the factor-of-two convention

Take natural units ℏ=c=1, fixed real `k≥0`, `M>0`, `τ>0`, `a≥0`, and

\[
i\dot\psi=H(t)\psi,\quad
H=m(t)\sigma_z+k\sigma_x,
\quad m=M[1-a\operatorname{sech}^2(t/\tau)].
\]

The state starts in the asymptotic negative-energy eigenspace and is projected onto the asymptotic positive-energy eigenspace. Let `E=√(m²+k²)` and `α=atan2(k,m)`. For `k>0`, a continuous angle is well-defined and the instantaneous basis rotates by α/2. The exact off-diagonal coupling generated by that rotation has magnitude

\[
|g|=\frac{|\dot\alpha|}{2}
=\frac{|k\dot m|}{2E^2}.
\]

Two dimensionless conventions must be distinguished:

\[
\eta_E=|g|/E=\frac{|k\dot m|}{2E^3},\qquad
\eta_{\rm gap}=|g|/(2E)=\frac{|k\dot m|}{4E^3}.
\]

The true eigenvalue gap is 2E and the transition phase accumulates at `2∫E dt`. Thus the proposed denominator `2E³` is correct for the explicitly defined coupling-to-E ratio; the textbook off-diagonal-coupling-to-gap ratio has `4E³`. The simpler diagnostic `|dot m|/E²` is sufficient to flag a rapid change but misses the directional factor k/E. Local smallness alone is not a general uniform theorem for arbitrary repeated histories or resonant driving. [Comparat's primary analysis](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0607118).

The exact second-order consequence is

\[
\ddot\psi+[E^2I+i\dot m\sigma_z]\psi=0,
\]

so its components satisfy `ü+(E²+i dot m)u=0` and `v̈+(E²−i dot m)v=0`. Dropping `±i dot m` turns it into a different problem. This supplies an independent algebra control for any implementation.

For `0≤a<1`, the mass never crosses zero. A useful sufficient bound follows from

\[
\max|\dot m|=\frac{4Ma}{3\sqrt3\tau},\qquad
E_{\min}^2=k^2+M^2(1-a)^2.
\]

Hence `max η_gap ≤ |k|Ma/(3√3 τ E_min³)`. This is a conservative local diagnostic, not an a priori guarantee that the final occupation equals zero. For a history with a nonzero gap, one exact global bound can be derived in the rotating frame. If `η=g/(2E)` is signed, integration by parts in the exact coupled-amplitude equation gives

\[
|\beta|\le |\eta(t_i)|+|\eta(t_f)|
+\int_{t_i}^{t_f}|\dot\eta|dt
+\int_{t_i}^{t_f}\frac{g^2}{2E}dt.
\]

Unitarity bounds the other amplitudes by one. All terms would need actual enclosures before calling a numerical use of this inequality certified. Unlike a pointwise diagnostic, it explicitly accounts for the whole history.

## 4. Zero momentum and two crossings are essential controls

At k=0, all Hamiltonians commute. The initially occupied σz eigenvector changes only by a phase. Since the mass is the same positive M at both asymptotic ends, **the exact final positive-energy occupation is zero for every a and τ**, including two mass crossings. An instantaneous positive/negative labeling becomes singular at a zero of m, but the evolution does not. A diagnostic that diverges at the crossing does not prove particle production.

For a>1 there are two mass zeros,

\[
t_\pm=\pm\tau\operatorname{arcosh}\sqrt a,\qquad
v=|\dot m(t_\pm)|=\frac{2M}{\tau}\sqrt{1-1/a}.
\]

For sufficiently separated, locally linear avoided crossings, a single-crossing Landau–Zener approximation gives `p≈exp(−π k²/v)` in this Hamiltonian convention. The double passage includes an interference phase; in a consistent scattering-phase convention it has the form

`n≈4p(1−p) sin²(ζ+φ_S)`, with `ζ=∫(t₋ to t₊) E dt`.

The phase shift can appear as a cosine or an oppositely signed Stokes phase under different definitions. The invariant message is the phase-sensitive envelope `0≤n≤4p(1−p)` within the approximation. Two large local transitions can cancel. Do not present a product of isolated-crossing probabilities as an exact result for the full pulse. Double-crossing interference is established physics. [Shevchenko, Ashhab and Nori](https://arxiv.org/abs/0911.1917).

The limiting controls are `a=0 ⇒ n=0`, `k=0 ⇒ n=0`, and norm conservation `0≤n≤1`. Fermionic preheating and its Pauli-limited mode occupations are established research, including nonperturbative excitation of heavy fermions. [Greene and Kofman](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-ph/0003018). This toy neglects expansion, backreaction, interactions and degeneracy accounting; a single mode's occupation is neither a total energy density nor a reheating temperature.

\[
b_1=\frac{aMk}{E_0}\left|\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}
\operatorname{sech}^2(t/\tau)e^{2iE_0t}dt\right|
=\frac{2\pi aMk\tau^2}{\sinh(\pi E_0\tau)}.
\]

For completeness, the substitution `x=exp(2t/τ)` changes the Fourier integral into

`2τ ∫₀∞ x^(iE₀τ)/(1+x)² dx = 2τ Γ(1+iE₀τ)Γ(1−iE₀τ)`.

The Gamma reflection identity yields the displayed result. No stationary matching coefficient is used here.

Let `λ=∫||V(t)||dt=2aMτ`. Twice iterating the exact interaction-picture integral equation, while retaining the exact unitary propagator in the remainder, bounds its operator norm by the ordered double integral `λ²/2`. Consequently,

\[
\max(0,b_1-2a^2M^2\tau^2)^2
\le n_k\le
\min\{1,(b_1+2a^2M^2\tau^2)^2\}.

This is an exact inequality for the prescribed two-level pulse, not an assertion that first-order perturbation theory is exact. It is useful where the lower bound is positive; otherwise it is inconclusive.

## 6. Exact stationary cancellation does not forbid this production

Take two degenerate Majorana modes and one light Yukawa row,

`M(φ)=m(φ)I₂`, `Y=y(1,i)`, with real m and constant y. Then, wherever m≠0,

\[
C_5=\frac{y^2}{m}(1+i^2)=0,\qquad
\frac{dC_5}{d\phi}=0.
\]

Every stationary lepton-number-violating kernel of the degenerate form `Y f(M)Yᵀ` vanishes for the same reason, including its finite-momentum pole expression. The two Majorana fields can be combined into a Dirac pair; the cancellation expresses lepton-number conservation. It is not a cancellation of the heavy Hilbert space or of energy absorption from a changing mass.

A common real mass pulse preserves this lepton-number symmetry yet can produce a particle–antiparticle pair. The explicit noncrossing pulse in Section 5 has a strictly positive mode-transition bound while **C₅ and its derivative vanish at every time**. Thus a C₅-based portal screen would miss this process. A zero classical fermion mean field does not force its quantum mode occupation to vanish; the Bogoliubov/mode problem and a classical linear forcing term are different objects. This construction creates no net lepton asymmetry and does not establish baryogenesis. Physical flavor and spin degeneracies must be counted consistently before converting its two-level occupations into number or energy density.

This is a concrete logical boundary for combining N00AK's lepton-number-violating kernel algebra with a blast hypothesis. It does not refute the static algebra, restore the unresolved loop coefficient, derive a cosmological action, or show that the registered HDBLAST scalar is this mass-modulating field.

The exact comparison is in the quadratic heavy-field model with a test LH current: Y defines its tree response to that current, and the current is zero during mode evolution. A full interacting Standard Model with nonzero dynamical Yukawa couplings also permits scattering and decay. Those effects are omitted by the stated toy approximation, so its exact mode bound is not an all-orders interacting-SM production theorem. The symmetry still explains the lepton-number-violating tree cancellation.
