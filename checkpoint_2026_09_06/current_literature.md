# TOE literature update: EFT matching, neutrino observables and optional cosmology checks

Prepared 6 September 2026. Cutoff: publications available by 6 September 2026. This is a targeted primary-source search, not an exhaustive survey of the web. Exact arXiv submission dates and selected full-text equations were checked. Recent theoretical preprints are research leads; none is empirical confirmation of HDBLAST, extra dimensions, or a unified theory.

The initial search covered the cosmology language in the request. The user subsequently clarified that this task continues the TOE work, with “higher dimensional blast” to be disregarded here. The primary references for that work are entries 4, 9 and 10: dimension-seven operator mixing, complete running and basis reduction. Entry 3 offers a recent tree-level matching benchmark. The scalar-production and cosmology references are retained as optional applicability examples, not as an adopted or calibrated TOE model.

## What materially changes the next calculation

The central TOE task is to evaluate the derivative LLHH mixed bubble with the direct and crossed momentum routings, then perform the sourced equation-of-motion reduction consistently. The literature supplies the relevant operator bases and running, but no source checked here independently verifies the disputed N00AK coefficient. A new contribution must arise from a completed calculation or a new, demonstrably distinct relation; reusing known seesaw or reheating formulas is not a new result.

Two useful 2026 developments for TOE are explicit scalar–seesaw matching in two threshold orders and the connection of dimension-seven operators to neutrinoless double-beta decay through operator mixing. Both stress the distinction between an intermediate coefficient and a measurable observable. Optional cosmology developments concern Pauli-limited fermion production and the separate accounting of photon entropy and dark radiation. None repairs the unresolved N00AK-r1 loop coefficient by citation.

## Ten-source shortlist

### 1. Neutrino reheating with nonthermal leptogenesis

**Xinyi Zhang, “Neutrino reheating predictions with non-thermal leptogenesis.”** First submitted **27 January 2025**; v3 **9 July 2025**, matching the published paper. JHEP **8 July 2025**, article 099.

Primary sources: [arXiv record](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.16114), [v3 full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.16114v3), [journal](https://doi.org/10.1007/JHEP07(2025)099).

**Evidence:** peer-reviewed theoretical calculation, not a detected mechanism. Direct inflaton–right-handed-neutrino coupling connects reheating duration, baryon asymmetry and inflationary observables in four specified models.

**Application:** carry the heavy-neutrino energy density explicitly. Infer the radiation-dominated temperature from the full history, rather than identifying a source-pulse timescale with a temperature. Compare the resulting baryon yield and expansion history jointly.

**Limit:** the baryon-asymmetry/spectral-index relation requires a specified inflationary potential and reheating model. It cannot be transferred unchanged to a generic bulk pulse. The intermediate regime requires Boltzmann evolution; the simple limiting formula is insufficient there.

### 2. Production of Majorana fermions by a changing background

**Weiyi Deng, Chengcheng Han, Wuzhou Yin and Tong Ju, “Right-Handed Neutrino Production by an Axion-like Inflaton: Implications for Leptogenesis.”** First submitted **15 July 2026**, v1 inspected.

Primary sources: [arXiv record](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.13592), [full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.13592v1).

**Evidence:** recent theoretical preprint. It treats derivative axion–fermion coupling, distinguishes particle bases, and includes repeated production, Pauli blocking and prompt/delayed decay.

**Application:** a fermionic mode population must obey $0\leq |\beta_r(k)|^2\leq1$. The paper's Eq. (87) evaluates number density by integrating this population with the $a^{-3}$ dilution factor. Earlier populations must enter later evolution as initial conditions; independently adding burst yields can overcount occupied modes. Eq. (110) gives the standard vacuum decay width $\Gamma_{N_i}=(Y_\nu^\dagger Y_\nu)_{ii}M_i/(8\pi)$.

**Limit:** this is a derivative axion coupling, not the same interaction as a real scalar mass modulation $M_N(s)$. Import the method and consistency checks, not its production spectrum. An evolving mass near a zero also invalidates integrating out that neutrino during the production episode.

### 3. A scalar–seesaw matching benchmark

**Carla Biggio, Marta Fuentes Zamoro, Xu Li, Luca Merlo and Luca Ottonello, “How to Identify a Majoron: Effective Field Theories of Spontaneous Lepton Number Breaking.”** First submitted **12 August 2026**, v1 inspected.

Primary sources: [arXiv record](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11522), [full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.11522v1).

**Evidence:** recent theoretical preprint. A complex scalar breaks a global symmetry; the radial mode is heavy and the angular mode is a Majoron. The authors integrate out the radial and seesaw states in both orders and compare the final low-energy theory.

**Application:** threshold-order agreement is an excellent independent test for a proposed scalar–neutrino bridge. A single symmetry-breaking scale can correlate several low-energy coefficients and observables instead of fitting each independently.

**Limit:** the paper explicitly states in its conclusion that matching is at tree level, and one-loop matching would generate absent operators and permit consistent running. Relations such as its Eq. (4.5), connecting invisible Higgs width and coupling suppression, require its field content and normalization. A real scalar with an explicit Majorana mass need not contain a Majoron or satisfy those relations. No coefficient here has been independently rederived for HDBLAST.

### 4. Dimension-seven operators can be visible through mixing

**J. de Vries, S. Fajfer, L. P. S. Leal, O. Sumensari and R. Zukanovich Funchal, “Neutrinoless Double-Beta Decays from Operator Mixing.”** First submitted **7 August 2026**, v1 inspected.

Primary sources: [arXiv record](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.07657), [full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.07657v1).

**Evidence:** recent theoretical/phenomenological preprint. It evolves the dimension-seven SMEFT coefficients and finds that operators absent from a tree-level decay amplitude can contribute after renormalization-group mixing. In some flavor channels a two-step mixing contribution is more important than the first leading logarithm.

**Application:** after obtaining trustworthy matching coefficients, propagate the complete coefficient vector to an actual low-energy observable. A cancellation in one intermediate expression is not evidence that every measurable amplitude cancels.

**Limit:** this does not supply the missing off-shell N00AK mixed-loop calculation, and does not establish that HDBLAST generates the operators analyzed. Experimental limits depend on flavor, interference assumptions, matching scale and nuclear matrix elements. Do not translate its single-operator bounds into a universal bound on a correlated model.

### 5. ACT DR6 provides a quantitative radiation check

**Erminia Calabrese et al., “The Atacama Cosmology Telescope: DR6 Constraints on Extended Cosmological Models.”** First submitted **18 March 2025**; full-text v2 **24 June 2025** inspected.

Primary sources: [arXiv record](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.14454), [v2 full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.14454v2).

**Evidence:** observational collaboration analysis. Section VI.1, Eq. (30), gives $N_{\rm eff}=2.86\pm0.13$ at 68% confidence for **P-ACT-LB**: Planck+ACT primary CMB, lensing and DESI Year-1 BAO. For an additional nonnegative free-streaming radiation component, Eq. (32) gives **$\Delta N_{\rm eff}<0.17$ at 95% confidence** with that combination.

**Application:** use the positive-component bound as an explicitly conditional screening value when the model leaves extra free-streaming relativistic energy and preserves the assumed thermal history.

**Limit:** the positive-component posterior is different from the unrestricted $N_{\rm eff}$ posterior. Subtracting 3.044 from an unrestricted upper limit produces a different inference. This constraint is not automatically applicable to interacting radiation, photon reheating, or the perturbations of an effective bulk Weyl term. The data provide no significant evidence for the new component.

### 6. Radiation cancellation does not remove entropy constraints

**Melissa Joseph, Jason Kumar and Pearl Sandick, “Constraints on the Injection of Radiation in the Early Universe.”** First submitted **7 April 2026**, v1 inspected.

Primary sources: [arXiv record](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.05282), [full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.05282v1).

**Evidence:** theoretical model and numerical likelihood analysis, using Planck 2018 and BBN abundances with LINX/PRIMAT. It examines mixtures of dark and electromagnetic radiation between BBN and recombination.

**Application:** photon heating and dark radiation can partly cancel in $\Delta N_{\rm eff}$, while photon heating changes the baryon-to-photon/entropy ratio. Track photon entropy, neutrinos, dark radiation and conserved baryon number separately. Its Eq. (3) defines $N_{\rm eff}^{\rm CMB}=\frac87(\frac{11}{4})^{4/3}(\rho_{\rm rad}-\rho_\gamma)/\rho_\gamma$.

**Limit:** the quoted approximately 25% relaxation concerns the paper's scenarios and priors, not a universal injected-energy bound. Nuclear-network differences matter. Its epoch is after BBN; an earlier completely thermalized pulse needs a different calculation. Do not multiply an ACT limit by 1.25 as if the analyses had identical likelihoods.

### 7. Updated gravitational-wave limits

**LIGO Scientific, Virgo and KAGRA Collaborations, “Cosmological and High Energy Physics implications from gravitational-wave background searches in LIGO-Virgo-KAGRA's O1-O4a runs.”** First submitted **30 October 2025**; v2 **7 November 2025** inspected.

Primary sources: [arXiv record](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.26848), [v2 full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.26848v2), [official collaboration record](https://dcc.ligo.org/LIGO-P2500150/public).

**Evidence:** observational collaboration search with no detected cosmological background. It constrains phase transitions, stiff expansion eras and other specified sources while including a compact-binary foreground.

**Application:** compare any derived spectrum against its complete spectral likelihood/constraints. A pulse-duration-to-peak-frequency estimate alone does not predict amplitude or establish detectability.

**Limit:** the phase-transition exclusion contours assume sound-wave or bubble-collision templates and stated priors. The paper explicitly warns that very small inverse durations approach the regime where bubbles are horizon-sized and the template's consistency is limited. No constraint in this paper is a model-independent observation of an extra dimension. A reheating portal need not make a first-order transition at all.

### 8. Existing nonthermal scalar–Majorana framework

**Xinyi Zhang, “Towards a systematic study of non-thermal leptogenesis from inflaton decays.”** First submitted **10 November 2023**; v2 **5 May 2024**, accepted in JHEP.

Primary sources: [arXiv record](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.05824), [v2 full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.05824v2).

**Evidence:** peer-reviewed theoretical foundation. Section 2 distinguishes prompt decay, neutrino domination, thermalization and strongly nonthermal limits.

**Application:** in its prompt-reheating, negligible-washout limit, Eq. (14) gives $Y_B=(3/2)(28/79)\epsilon T_{\rm RH}/M_\phi$, for unit branching into two neutrinos. Its kinematic requirement is $M_\phi\geq2M_1$. A nonunit branching fraction and later dilution must be accounted for separately. A delayed neutrino-dominated era has a different yield, Eq. (13).

**Limit:** the paper notes that the maximum temperature during reheating can exceed $T_{\rm RH}$. Consequently $T_{\rm RH}<M_1$ alone is not a universal proof that thermal production and washout were negligible throughout the history. Coupling normalizations also differ across papers; scalar widths must be derived from the chosen Lagrangian.

### 9. Dimension-five/six/seven cross-dimensional running

**Di Zhang, “Renormalization Group Equations for the SMEFT Operators up to Dimension Seven.”** First submitted **5 June 2023**; v2 **16 October 2023**, accepted in JHEP.

Primary source: [arXiv record and full-text links](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.03008).

**Evidence:** peer-reviewed theoretical calculation. It develops a Green's basis, a physical basis and reduction relations, and gives contributions to running through order $\Lambda^{-3}$, including effects involving lower-dimensional operators.

**Application:** use a declared basis, power counting, normalization and equation-of-motion prescription before comparing any N00AK or scalar-portal coefficient. This is a reference for reducing redundant derivative structures consistently.

**Limit:** renormalization-group equations do not determine the finite threshold matching of a new ultraviolet model, and are not a substitute for verifying the momentum routing and Wick contractions of a claimed loop amplitude. A field remaining light or being produced on shell cannot simultaneously be treated as a removed heavy SMEFT state during that event.

### 10. Complete same-dimension running and a reproducibility route

**Di Zhang, “Revisiting Renormalization Group Equations of the SMEFT Dimension-Seven Operators.”** First submitted **17 October 2023**; v2 **31 January 2024**, accepted in JHEP.

Primary source: [arXiv record, full-text and ancillary files](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.11055).

**Evidence:** peer-reviewed calculation of the nonredundant dimension-seven running, supplementing the cross-dimensional contributions above. The record supplies modified Matchmakereft ancillary files, and the paper reports partial checks with different tools and quantum-field gauges.

**Application:** benchmark a future implementation against the published basis and anomalous dimensions. Two independent routes—off-shell reduction and a complete operator calculation—would be substantially more informative than rerunning the same assumed cancellation formula.

**Limit:** this is established mathematics within effective field theory, not new project mathematics. Its ultraviolet examples and computational files need inspection before execution or adaptation. No ancillary code was executed in this literature search.

## Primary TOE integration sequence — project inference

1. Fix conventions for external momenta, LLHH operator normalization, flavor and weak-isospin contractions, regulator and subtraction scheme.
2. Compute the direct and crossed off-shell loop contributions separately before imposing special external kinematics. Retain local momentum polynomials and the full operator content needed for a physical reduction.
3. Compare the resulting redundant structures with the published Green's and physical bases. Include the relevant sourced equations of motion; a lower-dimensional equation of motion substituted into a higher-dimensional operator can change terms at the order under study.
4. Distinguish the UV divergence, the anomalous dimension, finite threshold matching and the eventual low-energy amplitude. Agreement in one does not certify all four.
5. Once coefficients are trustworthy, evolve their correlated vector to a selected observable such as a neutrino mass correction or lepton-number-violating amplitude. Keep exact tree identities and conditional loop algebra labeled separately.

## Optional scalar-production application — not the selected TOE model

1. Declare one portal and its symmetries. A real scalar modulation and a complex symmetry-breaking scalar are different models; only the latter automatically brings an angular degree of freedom into the discussion.
2. Separate the production calculation from the late-time effective theory. During a nonadiabatic event use the actual heavy-neutrino fields; after a valid hierarchy reappears, match to the low-energy operators. Avoid integrating out a field near an on-shell production threshold or mass crossing.
3. Evolve the scalar/source energy, neutrino distribution, radiation, any decoupled light sector, and the charge asymmetry. Include decay-time dilation for a nonthermal relativistic distribution. Verify total energy transfer and nonnegative populations.
4. Apply baryon-yield, dark-radiation and thermal-history conditions to the same parameter point. Report a screen as a screen; it is not a likelihood fit or a discovery. If a light Majoron is included, calculate its population instead of assuming it is negligible.
5. Only then derive the perturbations or gravitational-wave source. Establishing energy transfer into ordinary particles does not by itself establish a nearly scale-invariant perturbation spectrum or a distinguishable higher-dimensional signature.
6. Keep the N00AK-r1 distinction intact: exact tree identities remain useful, while the unresolved physical loop matching must be computed independently. Recent papers do not convert conditional cancellation algebra into an established physical coefficient.

The main near-term deliverable is a reproducible off-shell loop calculation with declared conventions and independent controls. The scalar-production calculation is a secondary illustration of the limits of low-energy coefficients: it does not select a cosmological TOE completion. A candidate survives only the tests actually applied and still requires model comparison and independent review.
