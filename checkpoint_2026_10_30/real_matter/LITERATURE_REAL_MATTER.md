## Scalar and fermion field-space geometry

Benoît Assi, Andreas Helset, Julie Pagès and Chia-Hsien Shen, [Renormalizing Two-Fermion Operators in the SMEFT via Supergeometry, arXiv:2504.18537v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.18537v1), 25 April 2025. The introduction organizes higher-dimensional scalar kinetic interactions into a metric on scalar field space and explains the use of field-space curvature as a covariant quantity. Sections 2 and 3 extend the framework to fermions and mixed boson-fermion loops. Section 5 describes a Green-to-physical reduction cross-check using both explicit relations with fermionic corrections and Matchete. Appendix B discusses covariance under the declared field changes.

**Use here:** the lepton normalization and radial scalar change are nonderivative, perturbatively local substitutions. Their residual scalar two-derivative interaction can be interpreted as a selected contribution to a field-space metric. Computing its curvature tests whether that selected metric is removable throughout a neighborhood by a scalar point-coordinate change; normalization at a single background does not establish a flat metric. This project must calculate that metric, its normalization and its curvature independently. The paper does not give the retained-sterile derivative-seed coefficient, and no coefficient or anomalous dimension is borrowed from it.

The paper's conclusion explicitly identifies difficulties with higher-derivative operators and changes that mix derivative orders. Accordingly, its standard field-space treatment does not complete this repository's original dimension-seven derivative/EOM reduction, a quantum composite-source map or the full retained-sterile operator basis. Curvature of this selected field-space metric is also distinct from spacetime curvature and from a measured gravitational effect.

## Retained sterile fields and redundant coefficients

Di Zhang, [Two-loop Renormalization Group Equations in the νSMEFT, arXiv:2504.00792](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.00792), first posted 1 April 2025; inspected metadata reports an update on 7 May 2025. Three provider-selected in-body passages from the renormalization discussion, Section 6 and conclusions were read; their served body was unversioned. They discuss apparent divergent field and Yukawa RG functions, redundant-operator contributions to derivatives of renormalization constants, and flavor transformations of the coupling matrices.

**Use here:** retain the dependence on redundant coordinates through RG differentiation and treat flavor-covariant Yukawa matrices separately from flavor invariants. An explicit classical normalization/source map is a prerequisite for that collection, rather than a total beta function by itself. The reference has retained right-handed neutrinos, making its field-content caution directly relevant, but its calculation is through dimension five and does not furnish this dimension-seven seed's answer. This reference had already been inspected in earlier checkpoints; the selected passage read here refreshes the specific RG warning.

## Mixed-dimension reduction

Di Zhang, [Renormalization Group Equations for the Dimension-7 SMEFT Operators, arXiv:2409.02622v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.02622v1), 4 September 2024. The versioned primary body was retrieved from the ordinary-web result. The introduction and Section 2 explain that dimension-five interactions make some redundant dimension-six operators enter dimension-seven reduction. The displayed running structure includes contributions involving different operator dimensions.

**Use here:** retain the conditional insertion of an independently supplied `C5` when applying a normalization whose generator has mass dimension `-2`. Such a term is a mixed-dimension action contribution, not a claim that the selected seed loop generated an independent `C5`. Pure SMEFT has different field content from the retained-sterile theory used here; its basis and coefficients cannot simply replace the latter.

## Truncation and induced interactions

Rodrigo Alonso, Christoph Englert, Wrishik Naskar and Shakeel Ur Rahaman, [Assessing (H)EFT theory errors by pitting EoM against Field Redefinitions, arXiv:2511.15609](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.15609), first posted 19 November 2025; inspected metadata reports an update on 30 March 2026. Three provider-selected in-body passages cover an explicit Higgs change, the induced interactions and subsequent canonical normalization, together with conclusions on the effect of EFT truncation. These passages were unversioned.

**Use here:** removing a displayed derivative monomial requires carrying its induced potential, Yukawa and source interactions and declaring the retained order. This HEFT case study does not provide an uncertainty estimate for the present seed, and higher powers of a loop-pole field change are outside this first-order calculation.
