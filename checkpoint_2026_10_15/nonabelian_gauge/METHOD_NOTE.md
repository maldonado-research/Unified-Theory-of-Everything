## Denner's identity is an effective-action statement

[Section 3, Eq. (23)](https://arxiv.org/html/hep-ph/9410338v2#S3.E23) writes the invariance of the background effective action, including gauge, scalar and ordered fermion variations. In its own Grassmann-derivative convention the fermionic contribution is ordered as

    delta(bar F) (delta Gamma/delta bar F)
       - (delta Gamma/delta F) delta F.

The sign and order cannot be replaced by commuting-source calculus. The surrounding text states that differentiating this functional identity yields background Ward identities, for arbitrary quantum-gauge parameter and to all perturbative orders. It explicitly distinguishes these identities from conventional gauge-fixed Slavnov–Taylor identities, whose structure generally involves ghosts.

Section 2.2 around Eqs. (21)-(22) distinguishes the vertex functions generated by Gamma from the subsequent construction of an S-matrix: the latter forms trees from Gamma vertices connected by background propagators. Section 3 after Eq. (24) also says that background-field gauge-fixing terms are not included in those vertex functions.

**Consequence for the selected calculation:** define which functional is being differentiated. For local 1PI vertices with retained external/background gauge fields, attaching a one-gauge loop vertex to a tree triple-gauge vertex through one gauge propagator produces a reducible connected graph. Cutting that gauge propagator disconnects it. It is not an additional independently local two-gauge 1PI residue.

A connected amplitude may need precisely such reducible contributions and the counterterms of its lower-point vertices. Excluding them from a stated 1PI kernel does not mean excluding them from every physical amplitude. If the gauge field itself were eliminated, the resulting matter-only nonlocal functional would be a different object and would need its own definition.

The non-Abelian lower-point adjoint term in a differentiated 1PI Ward identity already follows from the **nonlinear background gauge transformation** in Eq. (21). It is not a reason to insert a reducible triple-gauge tree into the 1PI loop list.

## The primary conventions differ from the task's Dplus convention

Denner's [Eq. (4)](https://arxiv.org/html/hep-ph/9410338v2#S2.E4) uses

    Dhat_mu = partial_mu - i g2 I_W^a What_mu^a
                            + i g1 (Y_W/2) Bhat_mu.

Its [Eq. (2)](https://arxiv.org/html/hep-ph/9410338v2#S2.E2) consequently has weak strength

    Fhat_mn^a = partial_m What_n^a - partial_n What_m^a
                                      + g2 epsilon^{abc} What_m^b What_n^c.

It uses opposite displayed SU(2) and hypercharge connection signs. Its Eq. (11) defines

    Zhat = cW What^3 + sW Bhat,
    Ahat = -sW What^3 + cW Bhat.

These signs are part of that convention; they cannot be imported individually into the task.

The modern paper's Section 2, Eqs. (1)-(4), instead uniformly uses

    D_mu psi = partial_mu psi - i g_A t^A V_mu^A psi,
    F_mn^A = partial_m V_n^A - partial_n V_m^A
                                   + g_A f^{ABC} V_m^B V_n^C,
    (D_r F_mn)^A = partial_r F_mn^A
                                   + g_A f^{ABC} V_r^B F_mn^C.

Its generators are Hermitian. These signs are mutually consistent. The modern paper is a general dimension-six template, not the present dimension-seven coefficient calculation.

### Direct conversion to Dplus

For the task's declaration

    D_mu = partial_mu + i A_mu,
    A_mu = g_y B_mu I + g_w W_mu^a T^a,
    [T^a,T^b] = i f^{abc} T^c,

a direct commutator, rather than a copied convention, gives

    [D_mu,D_nu] = i Fcal_mn,
    Fcal_mn = partial_mu A_nu - partial_nu A_mu + i[A_mu,A_nu],

and therefore

    Fcal_mn = g_y B_mn I + g_w T^a Wminus_mn^a,
    Wminus_mn^a = partial_mu W_nu^a - partial_nu W_mu^a
                                         - g_w f^{abc} W_mu^b W_nu^c.

For an adjoint-valued X, the same convention gives

    D_mu X = partial_mu X + i[A_mu,X],
    (D_mu X)^a = partial_mu X^a - g_w f^{abc} W_mu^b X^c.

Thus the quadratic weak-curvature sign in Dplus is negative for the stated Hermitian generators and structure constants. Reversing a source's gauge field while keeping the same generators gives a consistent conversion; simply replacing T by -T while retaining the same structure constants does not preserve the stated generator algebra.

For example, at fixed generators and couplings, Denner's weak connection converts by `What=-W_task`; its strength then equals `-Wminus_task`. Hypercharge converts separately according to its charge normalization and scalar choice. The task uses the conjugated scalar `phi=tilde H`, so the Higgs hypercharge assignment must also be translated. The Denner neutral rotation then agrees with the usual task rotation only after the weak-field conversion. No charged/neutral vertex sign should be transferred in isolation.

A compatible infinitesimal convention is

    psi' = exp(-i omega) psi,
    delta A_mu = partial_mu omega + i[A_mu,omega].

With omega=g_w alpha^a T^a this yields

    delta W_mu^a = partial_mu alpha^a
                         - g_w f^{abc} W_mu^b alpha^c,
    delta psi = -i g_w alpha^a T^a psi.

Differentiating the corresponding functional identity produces the endpoint-generator and adjoint lower-point terms. Their final momentum signs must be obtained using the actual incoming-momentum Fourier convention and fermion derivative order. This note does not substitute a convention-free momentum-space Ward formula.

## Multi-gauge completion: what follows and what remains to calculate

The modern paper's Eq. (6) uses the **full** non-Abelian field strength in its dipole monomial, with sigma defined in Eq. (8) as `i[gamma^mu,gamma^nu]/2`. A linear-gauge piece and its quadratic-curvature completion therefore belong to one operator after convention conversion.

For two labelled connection modes `X1 e1_mu` and `X2 e2_mu`, the Dplus curvature's bilinear part is

    Fcal_mn^(12) = i[X1,X2]
                       (e1_mu e2_nu - e1_nu e2_mu).

Both antisymmetric Lorentz slots are present; contraction with an antisymmetric sigma matrix produces the corresponding factor of two. This is an operator-expansion relation, not a new two-W loop integration.

Because the hypercharge matrix is central on a fixed weak representation,

    [g_y I, g_w T^a] = 0.

Thus this curvature commutator has **no direct mixed-BW component**. That fact does not imply that the selected mixed-BW 1PI kernel vanishes. Fundamental covariant derivatives, scalar seagulls and derivative descendants can contain mixed terms. For example

    D^2 = Box + i(partial.A) + 2i A.partial - A^2

has a mixed connection term proportional to `-{X_B,X_W} e_B.e_W`, even though `[X_B,X_W]=0`. A contact from the covariant derivative multiplying a field strength must also be retained when expanding its full operator.

For two W fields, retain ordered products and decompose their symmetric/antisymmetric parts only after including all placements. The commutator obeys the adjoint functional Ward identity, while the anticommutator contributes independent ordering information. For mixed BW, the Abelian and weak contractions still act on their respective charged matter/scalar endpoints; the absence of a mixed structure constant removes only the adjoint commutator term.

Ward identities constrain longitudinal relations. In general they do not determine every transverse local polynomial. Any claimed unique completion requires the actual local degree/dimension bound and an explicit classification of the remaining kernel space, or a direct loop calculation. None of the primary sources supplies the selected seed-Yukawa two-W or mixed-BW coefficient.
